# Expedition Ship POV Audit — 2026-08-21

## Scope

This is a camera/evidence audit of the existing 27/27 checkpoint. It does not reopen or extend the stopped geometry correction loop.

Evidence: `.img2threejs/expedition-ship/evidence/pov-audit-2026-08-21/`

The lab now exposes deterministic camera presets for:

- exterior front, side, and rear orbit views;
- interior overview, entry-deck, garden-floor, sky-terrace, and travel-interior cutaways;
- roof-systems and lower-drive views.

## Findings

1. **The front view overstates the current fidelity.** The controller outline reads from the front, but the 90-degree view collapses to an extremely thin slab. The main hull needs real fore-aft volume and a continuous shoulder-to-grip side silhouette.
2. **The inhabited core is a facade, not yet an interior.** The tree, deck rails, lamps, and room hints are staged to read through the front aperture. Cameras placed inside collide with the tree, structural bars, or back wall because there is no navigable room volume.
3. **The rear is under-authored.** The back view exposes a large empty opening and placeholder central mechanism rather than a resolved pressure hull, rear shield/window, circulation, and propulsion spine.
4. **The roof reads as stacked caps.** The top view lacks a coherent aerodynamic/compression shell, shield tracks, radiator/service zones, and convincing transformation seams.
5. **The underside communicates walking machinery, but not a complete tri-drive ship.** The centre propulsion unit is visually small and disconnected from the two handle drives; the belly/garage and load paths are not legible.

## Interior POV correction

The first audit incorrectly labelled several close exterior cutaways as interior viewpoints. They were still cameras in front of the pressure window. The corrected lab now distinguishes the `Cutaway overview` from genuine camera positions:

- `Under tree POV` places the camera on the sanctuary floor beside the trunk;
- `Canopy POV` stands beside the tree and looks upward;
- `Balcony looking down` places the camera above the upper starboard gallery floor and aims into the garden;
- `Balcony looking across` uses the same upper-gallery position and looks across the inhabited core.

These true probes are intentionally not beautified. They show that the camera intersects or is immediately blocked by solid facade beams, shallow room boxes, rails, ceiling masses, and foliage. This is direct evidence that the present interior is not a traversable spatial model. See `true-interior-pov-probes.png` in the evidence folder.

## Decision for the next bounded model cycle

Start with macro volume and spatial architecture, not Zen-garden decoration.

### P0 — volume lock

- deepen the central pressure hull and both controller grips until front, side, rear, roof, and lower views all hold;
- establish a closed rear silhouette and a continuous underside keel;
- keep the PS5-controller hand shape unmistakable in every transport pose.

### P0 — inhabitable core blockout

- create an actual walkable sanctuary floor around the protected tree clearance volume;
- add continuous balcony slabs, stairs/lifts, side galleries, room thresholds, ceiling clearance, and an aft circulation route;
- make the front shield/window retractable while preserving a sealed pressure-hull state.
- validate every floor and balcony with a human-eye-height camera placed inside the space; no camera may start inside solid geometry or require an exterior cutaway to read the room.

### P1 — propulsion and transformation coherence

- enlarge and structurally connect the centre drive with the two handle engines;
- show where the handle drives hinge into walking/hover/jump legs;
- give the rear and roof visible transformation tracks for atmospheric, fast-space, and hyper-compression modes.

### P1 — interior identity

- only after the walkable volumes hold, refine the Zen garden, living rooms, tea terraces, warm lighting, greenery, and micro-machine detail.

## Required evidence for the next cycle

Do not accept another front-only improvement. Each geometry checkpoint must include:

- exterior: front, 90-degree side, rear, roof, and underside;
- interior: overview plus at least one camera positioned within a real walkable space;
- a contact sheet showing all views at the same checkpoint.
