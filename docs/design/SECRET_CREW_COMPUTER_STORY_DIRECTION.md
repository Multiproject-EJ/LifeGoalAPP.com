# Secret Crew / Compass Computer story direction

Status: **Concept direction — compatible with current Island Run canon**

## Player premise

The player is a newly recruited member of the Compass Expedition. They do not
begin with a full explanation. They receive a summons, a role, a ship, and the
next instruction. Knowledge is earned through action and discovery.

The story camera stays first-person:

- never establish the player's age, body, gender, or face;
- hands, sleeves, boots, shadows, and reflections may appear when useful;
- characters address the player by chosen captain name;
- the player is invited into responsibility, never represented as physically
  forced or unable to leave the app.

The opening tone is safe, beautiful, and cozy. The mission gradually becomes
stranger as signals contradict the official explanation of the Great Drift.

## Secrecy rhythm

Mystery should create anticipation without hiding rules the player needs:

- gameplay costs, rewards, probability, purchases, and save behaviour stay
  clear;
- story identity, creature contents, unseen islands, motives, and signal
  origins may remain sealed;
- each island answers one meaningful question and creates one sharper question;
- reveals are attached to completed actions, not arbitrary waiting walls;
- skipped story can always be reopened from the log.

Use five reveal layers:

1. **Assignment** — what the player must do now.
2. **Local truth** — what this island believes happened.
3. **Contradiction** — evidence that does not fit.
4. **Transmission** — the Computer or an unknown intelligence reacts.
5. **Route secret** — a clue that changes the meaning of earlier islands.

## The Compass Computer

Replace the idea of a generic “creature control” screen with a compact 3D
mission instrument: the **Compass Computer**.

It should feel like a physical object embedded in the ship/controller:

- dark navy enamel, warm brass edges, glass depth, restrained cyan signal;
- a rotating compass core rather than a flat menu icon;
- instructions arrive as cards projected from the core;
- creature signals appear as partial silhouettes/waveforms until unlocked;
- the player can reopen the current assignment, evidence log, crew channel, and
  sealed files;
- dice, essence, and event tickets are shown only when relevant to the current
  instruction, with exact costs.

The Computer is a narrative surface only. It reads canonical gameplay state and
routes the player to existing actions; it never grants rewards or writes stop
progress.

## Two-state video results

Selected story videos may have two authored outcomes:

- **Resonant result** — the player understood the signal or completed the
  optional real-life bridge.
- **Uncertain result** — the player proceeded without that evidence or chose to
  postpone.

Both states must continue the main story. The second is not punishment and
cannot remove paid or earned rewards. It may change the next line, clue angle,
character confidence, or which sealed file opens first.

Branch input must come from canonical, explainable state. Never infer a mental
health condition, moral worth, or sensitive identity.

## Opening sequence

1. Darkness, ship ambience, hands near a dormant Compass Computer.
2. A single brass point wakes and scans the player's chosen captain/ship names.
3. “Crew designation accepted. Route incomplete.”
4. First Light Shore appears through the glass.
5. The welcome cache is secured.
6. An egg-like signal interrupts the ordinary mission channel.
7. The Computer seals the file: “Origin unknown. Signal resumes after Island
   One.”
8. Control moves directly to the board.

This establishes comfort, role, secrecy, and a reason to finish Island 1
without dumping lore into onboarding.

## Safety and tone

The player should feel curious and needed, not manipulated or threatened.
Avoid addiction language in product copy, coercive urgency, fake social proof,
or making the island suffer because the user missed a habit. The fiction can
be urgent; the real-life relationship with the app remains gentle.
