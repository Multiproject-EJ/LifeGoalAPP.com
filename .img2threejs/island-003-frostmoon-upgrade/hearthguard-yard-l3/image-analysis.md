# Hearthguard Yard L3 image analysis

## Identification and suitability

The approved target is a stylized arctic outdoor training-yard landmark presented in one hero three-quarter view plus front, side and rear elevations. It is a complex real-time environment prop rather than a single building. Suitability is **pass** for a stylized procedural reconstruction: the footprint, open negative space, major assemblies, materials and hidden elevations are all visible. Exact board-world scale and route clearance remain governed by the existing Island 003 landmark socket.

## Overall form and silhouette

The landmark occupies a low oval stone plinth. Its dominant negative space is the open training court. Vertical mass is deliberately distributed around the perimeter: a front gate at medium height, a taller climbing frame at the rear-left, two target posts at the rear-middle and a compact recovery hut at the rear-right. The perimeter is approximately bilateral around the entrance axis, but the equipment and hut make the interior asymmetric.

## Macro, meso and micro hierarchy

- Macro: oval plinth/court; entrance gate; perimeter fence; climbing rig; target system; recovery hut; hearth/recovery system; rear service dressing.
- Meso: gate posts and feather crown; fence posts and rails; climbing uprights/crossbars/rings; two shield targets; hut shell/roof/chimney; bench/brazier/lantern; banner mast; wood and barrel storage.
- Micro: copper post caps and foot plates; rope lashings; shield rims/bosses; roof courses/rivets; feather veins; snow caps; lantern cages; stone joints; banner finial.

## Spatial relationships and attachments

- The court is embedded in the oval stone foundation.
- Gate posts are socketed into the front foundation and carry the feather crown and two lanterns.
- Fence posts are embedded around the perimeter; rope/timber rails overlap adjacent post sockets.
- The climbing frame is embedded into the rear-left court edge; its rings hang from the upper crossbar.
- Target posts are embedded along the rear edge and support shield plates at chest height.
- The recovery hut is embedded at the rear-right and owns the roof, chimney, interior lantern, bench and brazier sockets.
- Rear storage overlaps the hut's rear/service elevation without widening the landmark footprint.

## Materials and finish

- Charcoal stone: dielectric, roughness about 0.78, chipped/bevelled blocks and snow-filled joints.
- Dark timber: warm low-value brown, roughness about 0.68, visible beam construction and slightly lighter cut ends.
- Raw copper/bronze: metallic, roughness about 0.38–0.48, used for the gate feather, roof skin, caps, brackets, target rims and lanterns.
- Rope: muted tan dielectric, roughness about 0.9.
- Snow: cool high-value dielectric, roughness about 0.92, placed on upward-facing caps and windward roof courses.
- Amber light/fire: warm emissive windows, lanterns and brazier; no blue roof and no aurora-dependent identity.
- Banner cloth: muted burgundy, matte, with one simple copper circular training sigil.

## Identity-defining features

1. Open oval court with clear usable negative space.
2. Copper feather-crowned front gate.
3. Rear-left climbing frame with two hanging rings.
4. Paired shield-target dummies.
5. Compact warm recovery hut with brazier and bench.
6. Full perimeter fence with intentional opening at the gate.
7. Rear service elevation with chimney, wood and barrel storage.

## Uncertainty and inference limits

The goal sheet supplies enough multi-view evidence for all silhouette-critical sides. Fine roof riveting, exact rope knots and the banner emblem are inferred stylized details. The target's large oval sheet footprint must be compressed into the existing compact gameplay landmark envelope, so phone-scale readability takes precedence over literal prop counts.
