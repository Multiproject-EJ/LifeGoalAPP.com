# Honeycomb Kingdom source image analysis

Reference: `docs/visual-references/island-014-honeycomb-kingdom/014-source.png`

## Observation before inference

The source is a tall portrait mobile-game composite showing a floating city in
three-quarter view. The live UI and text occupy much of the frame and are not
geometry evidence. Behind them, one large central island supports a circular
gold-paved court and a dominant, vertically stacked palace. Four smaller
landmark sites sit around the court. The cliff breaks into layered hexagonal
golden ledges with dark rock seams, flower beds and multiple amber liquid falls.
Blue sky and cream clouds provide a strong cool counterfield. Distant hive
towers repeat at progressively smaller scale and lower contrast.

## Macro hierarchy

1. Floating honey-rock landmass with deep front cliff and suspended underside.
2. Circular civic/board court centered on a many-towered domed hive palace.
3. Four satellite landmark zones with distinct tall/low silhouettes.
4. Dense blue-sky/cloud depth populated by distant hive islets and bees.

## Meso structure

- Palace: broad stair, central arched portal, stacked octagonal/hexagonal drum,
  large amber dome, crown finial, four smaller side towers and repeated cell
  lattice/buttress rhythm.
- Terrain: honeycomb paving, hexagonal ledge edges, dark stone fractures,
  garden terraces, short stairs, small bridges and honey channels/falls.
- Satellites: egg/nursery form, low pollinator work court, broad trials court,
  domed/archive form. Exact source structures are partially hidden by UI cards.
- Background: tall narrow hive towers with domed/cell roofs on small floating
  platforms; bee silhouettes cross near/mid/far layers.

## Micro inventory candidates

- royal bee emblems and purple enamel panels;
- amber glazing/honey bulbs inside bronze lattice;
- hex-cell façade apertures and paving inlays;
- honey drips, pooled highlights and viscous fall ribbons;
- flower clusters in white, pink, purple and orange;
- leaf/vine tendrils over honey-rock ledges;
- lantern posts, railings and dark bronze trim;
- pollen flecks and small airborne bee silhouettes.

## Material interpretation

- Gold-bronze structural frames: high metalness, medium-low roughness, dark
  cavity response and warm upper-left highlights.
- Wax/cream stone: low metalness, high diffuse response, slightly soft surface.
- Amber/honey: translucent or emissive-leaning warm material with high-gloss
  surface and thick-liquid optical read; avoid full-screen bloom.
- Dark rock: rough brown-black fracture seams for value separation.
- Flowers/leaves: matte saturated accents, used as readable clusters rather
  than tiny individual geometry at distance.

## Identity-defining features

Critical: dominant royal hive palace; fractured floating honey-rock; blue/cloud
depth; visible honey falls; bee civilization/air traffic; hexagonal language.
Important: flower terraces; distant hive colony; gold/amber/cream material
hierarchy; purple royal accents; distinct satellite silhouettes.

## Hidden information and controlled inference

The UI obscures portions of every satellite and much of the court. Rear and
underside construction is not visible. Hidden sides will extend the visible
hex-cell buttress, dome, balcony, honey-channel and service-entry language;
confidence is medium for massing and low for exact ornament. The source's
approximate route, embedded labels and UI are explicitly excluded.

## Intended runtime use

Real-time browser world and five action-ready procedural landmark families,
with named roots, focus sockets, colliders, construction stages and reduced-
motion ambience. Target is stylized source-faithful reconstruction at phone
scale, not exact recovery of unseen geometry.
