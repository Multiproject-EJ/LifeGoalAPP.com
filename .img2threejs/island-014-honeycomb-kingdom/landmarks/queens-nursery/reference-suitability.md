# Queen's Nursery — reference suitability

Verdict: **conditional — admitted for stylized procedural reconstruction**

## Why it is usable

- The target identity is explicit: the Hatchery card supplies one obvious royal honeycomb egg emblem.
- Its macro silhouette, dominant gold/amber materials, raised hexagonal ribs, and dark recessed cells are readable.
- The egg is approximately rotational/bilateral, so a credible true-3D macro shell can be inferred from one source-facing view.
- The requested result is a stylized game landmark made from procedural Three.js forms, not exact mesh extraction or manufacturing geometry.
- The complete island image supplies unusually strong palette, lighting, density, floral, honey, and architectural context.

## Conditions and limitations

- The reference is a complete UI scene, not an isolated orthographic object view.
- The explicit Nursery emblem is small and does not reveal the exact in-world base, side, rear, entrance, or attachment topology.
- Exact hex-cell count and micro-ornament cannot be claimed as source-authored.
- Side/rear architecture must therefore be conservative inference, documented as such, and independently checked through the orbit gate.
- The canonical phone view remains the identity-authoritative view; invented rear detail cannot compensate for a weak egg read there.

## Reconstruction route

- Use procedural true-3D construction: ovoid shell, surface-conforming wax ribs/cells, architectural cradle/base, entrance, compact side pods, crown, and socketed honey drips.
- Do not use front-plane projection as the primary geometry solution. Projection may be skipped because it would preserve UI contamination and produce a facade rather than a buildable landmark.
- Validate the isolated landmark in front, 45°, side, rear, top-oblique, and canonical phone views.

## Missing evidence to track

- Exact side and rear design: high uncertainty.
- Exact in-world scale relative to Palace: medium uncertainty.
- Entrance/base design: medium uncertainty.
- Micro-detail and local roughness: medium uncertainty.
